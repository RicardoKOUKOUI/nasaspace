NASA Space Apps 2026 · The problem

Earth is 20 minutes away.

On a Mars transit a flight surgeon cannot answer in real time. Radiation, isolation, gravity, and a closed cabin change the body quietly. The crew needs a medical copilot that lives on board.

The solution

ASTRO-PULSE

An offline health console. Four axes, one Mission Readiness score, and a plan the astronaut can start before the link comes back.

Telemetry

Heart, bone and muscle, radiation, sleep and mood. One cockpit.

Cross-check

Combined drifts lower the score before a single alarm would.

Copilot

Shelter, ARED, sleep, fluids, breathing, or a crew check-in.

Demo switch

Nominal, solar storm, isolation. One click, no network.

What you will see

Three states. One crew.

  1. Nominal cruise stays READY.
  2. Solar storm: dose spikes, shelter protocol appears.
  3. Isolation: sleep, exercise, and bone fall together.
  4. The 3-question check changes the plan on the spot.

NASA evidence

Set-points, not a black box.

1.84 mSv / day

MSL RAD cruise dose equivalent. Zeitlin et al., Science 2013.

1–1.5% bone / month

NASA Human Research Program. We use 1.2% as the cruise set-point.

ARED, 60 min

Resistive exercise is the countermeasure when loading disappears.

8-hour sleep

The allotment NASA protects. The fatigue case is that window lost.

The 30-sol record is synthetic and labeled as such, so the console still runs with the link down. Not a medical device.

Earth impact

Same engine, no signal.

A clinic that cannot reach a physician has the same constraint as this crew. The offline score and the short action list transfer to rural health posts, including in Benin, and to polar or humanitarian teams.
